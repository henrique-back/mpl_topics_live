Microplastics Research Monitor

A living review of the scientific literature on microplastics and marine plastic pollution. Every week, newly published papers are added, sorted into research topics and located in the world's ocean basins.

    How this review works

    The review covers the scientific papers indexed in Scopus that mention marine debris, plastic pollution, microplastics, nanoplastics, microfibres or plastic pellets together with the sea, the coast, the ocean or sediments. It started from papers and grows every week.

    1. Collecting papers. Every Monday, the papers added to Scopus during the past week are fetched with their abstracts; duplicates and papers without an abstract are left out.
    2. Grouping papers into topics. Each abstract is turned into a numerical "fingerprint" of its meaning by a language model (), and papers with similar fingerprints are grouped into topics by a clustering algorithm (BERTopic with UMAP and HDBSCAN). The detailed topics are then merged, most similar first, into medium and broad topics. New papers join the topic of the most similar existing papers; the topics themselves are only rebuilt from scratch occasionally (see below).
    3. Naming topics. Each topic is named and described by an AI model (Claude), from its characteristic words and its most typical abstracts.
    4. Locating papers. Place names in each abstract are recognised automatically, looked up in the GeoNames gazetteer and assigned to the nearest ocean basin; a paper belongs to the basin most of its places fall in.
    5. Citations. Citation counts come from Scopus and are refreshed monthly. To compare papers of different ages, citations are divided by the paper's age, and papers younger than years are left out of citation analyses.

    How reliable are the topics?

    Versions of the topics

    The topics are rebuilt from scratch only when many new papers no longer fit them; each rebuild starts a new version. Between rebuilds, papers are added to the existing topics and past assignments never change.

    Limitations

    • Coverage. Only papers indexed by Scopus and matching the search are included; books, reports and papers in other databases are not.
    • Locations. Basins are inferred from the place names in abstracts, which can be missing, ambiguous (a city name shared by two countries) or about where the authors work rather than where they sampled. of papers name no ocean place at all.
    • Topics. Topics are statistical groupings of similar abstracts, not an expert classification. A paper belongs to a single topic even when it touches several, and about of papers fit no topic well and are left out of the topic charts.
    • Citations. Citation counts favour older papers and fields with more researchers; the median and Gini coefficient describe each topic's citations, not the quality of its research.
    • AI-written text. Topic names, descriptions and the weekly newsletter are written by an AI model () and published without human review. They can be inaccurate.
